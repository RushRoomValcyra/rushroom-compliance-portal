Decided 2026-10-07 after the first real use. A part never holds real children. When you press +child on a part there are exactly two choices — no questions, no long text, and the part's Sourcing does not matter.

Why “In the box” never touches stock below the part

Checked in the code, 2026-10-07: the only way BOM quantities leave the portal is the planner resolver to Order Operations (Order Operations reads nothing else), and it already skips In-the-box links — so the part stays one order line and one pick line. Work orders take their pull list from product families, not from the BOM tree. In-the-box rows appear only in the portal's own tree and exports, marked as such.

Move works the same way: moving a row onto a part in the same structure offers the same two choices. “Prepared assembly” builds it where the part sits and puts the moved row inside — e.g. the drawer front handle onto L Drawer Front - White gives Prepared L Drawer Front - White = front ×1 + handle.

What goes away: the “what is this child?” dialog, the “cannot hold children yet” dialog, the in-dialog fix buttons, and the Sourcing condition on a part's children. A part's children no longer depend on whether someone set its Sourcing correctly.

What stays: a finished good never gets +child · Hub/Site on In-the-box rows — decided 2026-10-07: some contents are pre-installed at the hub, some fitted on site · Sourcing is still chosen when a part is created.

1Every component answers three questions

All three already exist in the portal. The first two live on the component; the third lives on the work order, because it can differ for every customer. The proposal is to stop asking Type to answer all of them.

What is it?
Field: Type
  • partA single item — a panel, a switch, a screw
  • sub_assemblySomething that is a set of other items
  • Kit(stored as phantom_assembly) A pick group — never built or stocked as one thing, but its children are drawn from stock and picked together
  • finished_goodA complete product we buy and pass on untouched
  • product_familyDynamic BOM — a configurable product with variants
How does it come to exist?
Field: Sourcing (stored as make_or_buy)
  • PurchasedWe buy it complete
  • ManufacturedWe make it from material
  • AssembledWe put it together from bought parts
  • SubcontractedA supplier makes it for us, possibly from parts we provide or specify
What do we do to it?
Field: Operations (routing steps → work order steps)
  • drille.g. 2–10 extra holes, positions per customer
  • route / cutShape changes
  • insert / attachFittings pressed or screwed in
  • surface_treatFinish, lacquer
  • inspectChecks
The core idea: Type decides whether a component can have children. Sourcing decides what those children mean — are they things we build with, things the supplier builds with, or just a record of what's inside something we bought? Operations say what we do to it — and do not change what it is. A panel with two extra holes is still the same panel in the BOM.

6bRules we have decided

Agreed 2026-10-06. We work by these until real work shows one is wrong — then we correct it.

  1. Buy unit = BOM line.
    One thing we order and stock is one pick line, however many pieces arrive in the box. What comes with it is In the box: structured under it, never ordered or picked on its own, and it may carry Hub/Site where we fit it. Example: the LED profile (case D3), the drawer hardware set.
  2. A part never holds real children. Two ways to add to it: In the box (visual, no stock of its own) or a Prepared assembly (real structure, under Assemblies).
    The work we do to it lives in its Prepared … sub-assembly (Assembled), which the portal builds when you choose “Prepared assembly” (§0b). The test is one question: is it its own order line? Simplified 2026-10-07 (PROP-069): the part's Sourcing no longer matters. Applied to production by migration 0038: 24 parts, 42 links, 10 new wrappers.
  3. What we do to it is an operation, not structure.
    Customer holes, fitting a cover, attaching clips → steps on the work order. The BOM stays what we buy and pick.
  4. What it is made of is materials, not structure.
    Aluminium, plastic, zinc-plated steel → the Materials tab (RoHS / REACH), not child parts.
  5. A Kit is a pick group and is never dissolved.
    Never built or stocked as one thing; its children are drawn from stock and picked together under it. Stored as phantom_assembly, shown as Kit.
  6. Kits have no category.
    A category says what kind of physical thing an item is; a kit is deliberately mixed (panels, fasteners, Prepared wrappers). Which wardrobe module it belongs to (S / M / L / BB) is in its name for now. When filtering by module becomes painful, that is a Module field on assemblies — not a category.
  7. A finished good is always Purchased and never holds children.
    Hard block in the database. Matches PROP-029 and the 3 in production.
  8. Where value is added is said by Fitted, and only there: — nothing (picked as is) · Hub prepared / pre-assembled · Site fitted at the customer.
    Decided 2026-10-07 as a general principle. Every BOM link carries it, In the box included (some contents are pre-installed at the hub, some on site). The gap: operations — the “modified” half — cannot carry it yet (roadmap Next).
  9. Sourcing is chosen when a component is created — no default.
    The silent “Purchased” default is what made 90 of 91 parts untrustworthy.
  10. Manufactured and Subcontracted are decided when the first real case arrives.
    Production has none today. Questions 3 and 5 wait for that case — “until we hit the wall”. For a part, its inputs would go into a Prepared assembly like anything else.

7Decisions we need to make

These are the open points. Each one changes what gets built.

  1. Is this the right split — Type says whether it may have children, Sourcing says what they mean? Decided — yes
    Agreed 2026-10-06, with Operations as the third dimension (what we do to an item).
  2. Part + Manufactured, and Part + Assembled: what do we allow? Superseded — PROP-069
    A part never holds real children, whatever its Sourcing: In the box, or a Prepared assembly.
    Proposed: Manufactured may have inputs (sheet, paint); Assembled means it is really a sub-assembly. But a panel with inserts pressed in — is that a part we manufactured, or something we assembled?
  3. Do Manufactured and Assembled need to mean different things for a sub-assembly? Deferred to first case
    E.g. a welded frame (can't be taken apart) vs. a screwed unit (can). If the difference doesn't matter to us, the two can behave the same.
  4. Do we want to record what's inside things we buy? Decided — In the box
    First built as “reference children” that could not carry Hub/Site (PROP-067). Reworked 2026-10-07 (PROP-068) after the LED profile and drawer set showed contents arrive loose and are fitted by us: now In the box — never its own order or pick line, may carry Hub/Site, counts for compliance. Needs a marker on the link and the planner resolver must skip reference links (it would otherwise send them as pick lines).
  5. Deferred to first case For Subcontracted items: do we mark per line who supplies it (us / the supplier / the supplier, but our specification)?
    This is the one concept we don't have today. Needed now, or once the first subcontracted item exists?
  6. Phantoms: should the phantom level disappear on the pick list? Decided — no
    Phantoms are how we draw materials from stock and group the pick list, so the level is kept. Correction: the planner BOM resolver sends Order Operations leaves only, so the Kit grouping does not reach the pick list as a group yet — see the status board. This is where we deliberately differ from SAP/PTC, where a phantom is dissolved (“blown through”). Renamed in the UI to Kit (picked together, not built) — v282.
  7. Finished good: always Purchased, never children? Decided — yes
    Hard block in the database. Matches PROP-029.
  8. Sourcing becomes a required choice when creating a component, with no default. Decided — yes
    Without this, every new component is silently “Purchased” and the consistency check in section 5 gives false alarms.
  9. Customer holes (case D1): where do the hole positions come from, and who records that they were drilled? Partly decided
    Decided: the holes are an operation on the work order, not a part or a child (rule 3). Still open: the two questions below.
    The portal's work orders can carry a drill step with the customer's hole drawing attached. But the customer order lives in Order Operations. Does the drawing start there and flow to the work order, or the other way round? And is the drilling done at the Hub or on Site?
  10. The flag in 5b: may the user dismiss it and keep a purchased part with children? Superseded — two options in one click (§0b)
    Neither dismiss nor block: “we fit it” builds a Prepared wrapper automatically and the part stays as it is; “comes with its order” puts it In the box. See §5b for the proposed details.
    Dismissable is gentler but lets the 24-case pattern grow again. Must answer keeps the data honest but adds a step every time. A middle way: allow “keep as is”, but the row keeps its review badge until someone answers.
  11. Rename the four kits from “… Module Common Parts Assembly” to “… Kit”? Done
