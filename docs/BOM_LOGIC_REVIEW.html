1Every component answers three questions

All three already exist in the portal. The first two live on the component; the third lives on the work order, because it can differ for every customer. The proposal is to stop asking Type to answer all of them.

What is it?
Field: Type
  • partA single item — a panel, a switch, a screw
  • sub_assemblySomething that is a set of other items
  • phantom_assemblyA grouping on screen only — never exists physically as one thing
  • finished_goodA complete product we buy and pass on untouched
  • product_familyDynamic BOM — a configurable product with variants
How does it come to exist?
Field: Sourcing (stored as make_or_buy)
  • PurchasedWe buy it complete
  • ManufacturedWe make it from material
  • AssembledWe put it together from bought parts
  • SubcontractedA supplier makes it for us, possibly from parts we provide or specify
What do we do to it?
Field: Operations (routing steps → work order steps)
  • drille.g. 2–10 extra holes, positions per customer
  • route / cutShape changes
  • insert / attachFittings pressed or screwed in
  • surface_treatFinish, lacquer
  • inspectChecks
The core idea: Type decides whether a component can have children. Sourcing decides what those children mean — are they things we build with, things the supplier builds with, or just a record of what's inside something we bought? Operations say what we do to it — and do not change what it is. A panel with two extra holes is still the same panel in the BOM.

4Our real buying situations, modelled

The situations Rushroom actually meets, and the Type + Sourcing combination for each.

A · We buy a plain part
A shelf pin, a screw, a bare panel.
part + Purchased → no children
  • Shelf pin
The most common case: 96 parts.
B · We buy an assembly complete
An LED driver, a switch with its cable already on.
sub_assembly + Purchased → contents for reference
  • Seat to Door Switch
    • Door switch inside, as delivered
Today Seat to Door Switch is a part holding Door switch — it belongs here instead, if it arrives assembled.
C · We buy an assembly, with one part of ours in it
The supplier builds it, but one item is our design, which we provide or tell them to buy.
sub_assembly + Subcontracted → supplier's inputs, our line marked
  • Door module
    • Frame, hinges supplier's
    • Custom front ours
Needs a per-line “who supplies this” marker — see question 5.
D1 · We buy a part and customise it per customer
Predrilled panels get 2–10 extra holes, anywhere, different for every order.
part + Purchased → no children, plus an operation on the work order
  • Left Side Panel - White same part number for every customer
  • Work order (example)
    • Step 3 drill Left Side Panel · 6 holes · customer's hole drawing attached
The holes are not material and not a new part. The BOM stays clean; what was done to each delivered panel is recorded on its work order. The portal already has this shape: routing steps with drill, work-order steps tied to a component, each with a reference document.
D2 · We buy a part and always modify it the same way
The same cut-out on every unit, a standard variant we stock.
part + Manufactured → the bought part is its input
  • Panel, with cable cut-out own part number
    • Panel, as bought
Only worth a new part number when the result is identical every time and is stocked or reused. If it varies per order, it is D1.
E · We assemble it ourselves
Our own build, at the hub or on site.
sub_assembly + Assembled → build inputs, with Hub / Site
  • L Cabinet
    • L Plinth Hub
    • Door switch Site
The only case where Hub / Site carries real meaning.
F · We buy a finished product and never touch it
Resold or included as delivered.
finished_good + Purchased → no children
  • Standing makeup mirror with LED
3 in production today, all leaves.

5The built-in consistency check

Once Sourcing is trustworthy, the fields can check each other. This is how the portal would find the 24 problem cases by itself, instead of us sorting them by hand.

    • Seat to Door Switch ×2 Site

    • Shelf pin ×2 Site

Contradiction Something we buy complete cannot have parts we fit on site. One of the two fields is wrong.

→

The children don't belong to the panel. Move them one level up, next to it. Their Site badge stays.
  • L Cabinet

    • Seat to Door Switch Site
    • Shelf pin Site
b · The supplier delivers the plinth with them fitted
It is a purchased sub-assembly. Retype it; clear Hub/Site on its children.
c · The supplier fits parts that we give them
Sub-assembly, Subcontracted. Mark which lines come from us.
d · We build a plinth unit from a bought panel
Make “L Plinth unit” a sub-assembly, Assembled; the bought panel becomes one of its children.

The rule

IF parent Sourcing = Purchased AND a child is marked Hub or Site → flag it
IF parent Sourcing = Subcontracted AND a child is marked Hub or Site → flag it

Shown as a warning in the tree and in a review list — not a hard block, because the fix needs a person who knows what actually happens in the workshop.

6Where the rule is enforced

Today five different code paths can attach a child to a component. Each applies its own rule, or none.

How a child gets attachedRule applied todayStatus
+child / +sib in the BOM treeOnly sub-assembly and phantom may be parents (added 2026-10-05)◐ Too strict — blocks Dynamic BOMs and manufactured parts
Move a child to another parentOnly “not a finished good”✕ Lets a part receive children
Copy an assemblyNone✕ No check
Materialise a Dynamic BOM configurationNone✕ No check
Change Type on the edit formNone — a sub-assembly with children can become a part✕ No check
DatabaseOnly the loop check (a component cannot contain itself)✕ No structure rule
Proposed: one rule in the database itself, so all five paths obey it automatically and no future screen can bypass it. The matrix in section 2 becomes that rule. It switches on only after the 24 existing cases are cleaned up, otherwise it would reject today's data.

7Decisions we need to make

These are the open points. Each one changes what gets built.

  1. Is this the right split — Type says whether it may have children, Sourcing says what they mean?
    Everything below depends on this one.
  2. Part + Manufactured, and Part + Assembled: what do we allow?
    Proposed: Manufactured may have inputs (sheet, paint); Assembled means it is really a sub-assembly. But a panel with inserts pressed in — is that a part we manufactured, or something we assembled?
  3. Do Manufactured and Assembled need to mean different things for a sub-assembly?
    E.g. a welded frame (can't be taken apart) vs. a screwed unit (can). If the difference doesn't matter to us, the two can behave the same.
  4. Do we want to record what's inside things we buy?
    For compliance it is valuable — substance declarations (RoHS, REACH) concern the contents. But it is extra work per purchased assembly. Optional, required, or not at all?
  5. For Subcontracted items: do we mark per line who supplies it (us / the supplier / the supplier, but our specification)?
    This is the one concept we don't have today. Needed now, or once the first subcontracted item exists?
  6. Phantoms: when we send a BOM to Order Operations, should the phantom level disappear, with its children attached to the level above?
    Today the planner BOM resolver sends the phantom as its own level. Matters as soon as Order Operations makes pick lists from it.
  7. Finished good: always Purchased, never children?
    Matches the existing decision (PROP-029). Confirm it still holds.
  8. Sourcing becomes a required choice when creating a component, with no default.
    Without this, every new component is silently “Purchased” and the consistency check in section 5 gives false alarms.
  9. Customer holes (case D1): where do the hole positions come from, and who records that they were drilled?
    The portal's work orders can carry a drill step with the customer's hole drawing attached. But the customer order lives in Order Operations. Does the drawing start there and flow to the work order, or the other way round? And is the drilling done at the Hub or on Site?

Cross-checked against how SAP, PTC Windchill and automotive directed-buy arrangements handle the same situations. They also keep “is it an assembly” separate from “do we make or buy it”, and treat bought assemblies, subcontracting and phantoms much as above. That is used here only to check the reasoning, not as a template — the Sourcing-gives-meaning rule and the Hub/Site consistency check are our own.
